01

Team of 5 · Project lead · GIS & spatial data science · Jul–Sep 2026

StaSIUN: valuing ad space, tenants and naming rights across 45 Jakarta KRL stations

Favorite Winner · MAPID WebGIS Competition 2026

The problem

Non-farebox revenue is only about 4% of KAI's total income, while MRT Jakarta, with far fewer passengers, reaches 30–40%. The gap isn't because the stations are weak assets: the value of their commercial space (ad space, tenant spots and naming rights) has simply never been measured spatially. Ad space shows it most clearly. Nobody can say who actually walks through a given station (its footfall persona) or how crowded it gets across time windows, so an ad spot can't be priced by what it really delivers. Small businesses around a station are just as blind: they can't see latent demand or how crowded the competition is.

The approach

  • Walking isochrones on the street network instead of circle buffers, because pedestrians can't walk through rail lines, rivers or arterial roads.
  • SEPI, a composite score over five variables (transport, economy, access, urban form, commercial), weighted with Entropy + AHP (consistency ratio 0.0072).
  • Uncertainty made explicit: bootstrap BCa intervals, and shrinkage toward stations of the same LDA archetype where survey data is thin. Every score carries its sample size and a confidence flag.
  • Ranking robustness tested across 5 weighting schemes and 1,000 weight perturbations.
  • Two-layer extraction of ads, tenants and facility complaints from 1,034 field-survey notes, capped at 15% of any score and cross-checked spatially.
  • An assistant that answers questions by calling the scoring engine itself (tool calling), so every answer traces back to a computed number.

The result

All 45 stations are scored and classed: 2 Premium Transit Hubs, 32 Moderate and 11 Low, led by Tanah Abang (76.5) and Sudirman (76.1). The isochrones changed the picture: at Sudirman the area actually reachable in a 10-minute walk is 0.92 km², against 2.18 km² for the circle a buffer would assume. Verified facility complaints were turned into 19 sponsorship opportunities at 10 stations. The team won Favorite Winner.

What's next

The ranking is stable across weightings, but the transport indicator is still thin: ridership data covers only 10 stations. The next step is full ridership and real ad-rate benchmarks, so the index can move from a ranking to a price.

02

Group project · Text Mining course · NLP · Jun 2026

Financial News Miner: sentiment toward each company in a headline

The problem

Headline-level sentiment misses the point. "Gold shines on seasonal demand; Silver dull" is good news for gold and bad news for silver. In the SEntFiN dataset, 2,850 of 10,753 headlines mention more than one entity and 1,235 of them carry conflicting sentiment. A model that scores the whole headline fails exactly where the signal matters.

The approach

  • Data: parsed the annotated SEntFiN 1.1 into 14,409 headline–entity pairs and checked that every entity appears verbatim, so NER tags could be generated by span matching.
  • Entity recognition: fine-tuned BERT (cased), scored at entity level with seqeval, where a multi-word entity only counts if the whole span is right.
  • Sentiment per entity: the model reads the headline and the entity together, as [CLS] headline [SEP] entity [SEP]. Compared FinBERT with class weights against plain BERT.
  • Honest baselines: CRF with hand-crafted features for NER and TF-IDF + linear SVM for sentiment. No LLM-generated labels, since the lecturer ruled them out as circular.

The result

Entity F1 rose from 0.837 to 0.915 and sentiment macro-F1 from 0.767 to 0.878. The real test is conflicting headlines: accuracy there went from 0.616 to 0.857, shrinking the gap between single-entity and conflicting headlines from 0.206 to 0.035. The model reads the entity, not the mood of the headline.

The trade-off

The transformer has about 110M parameters against 81K for the SVM, and takes 3.96 ms per pair on GPU versus 0.48 ms on CPU. For a machine without a GPU the SVM is still a sensible fallback; for mixed headlines, FinBERT is worth the cost.

Paper with Kevin Tan · Operations research · Jun 2026

Food rescue routing: vehicle routing with time windows, solved by simulated annealing

The problem

Food rescue programs collect surplus food from restaurants, hotels and supermarkets and bring it to people who need it. The food spoils, so every stop has a time window, and every vehicle has a capacity. That makes it a Vehicle Routing Problem with Time Windows (VRPTW), which is NP-hard: exact solvers only work for small instances.

The approach

  • Model: minimize total distance, with each customer visited once, capacity Q = 200 and arrival inside [ei, li].
  • Starting point: a nearest-neighbor route that already respects capacity and time windows.
  • Three moves: 2-opt inside a route, relocating a customer to another route, and swapping customers between routes. Every candidate is checked for feasibility before it is scored.
  • Cooling: geometric, from T0 = 1000 to 0.1 with α = 0.9995, up to 50,000 iterations, on the Solomon C101 benchmark (100 customers).

The result

Total distance dropped from 2,119.00 to 1,698.57 (−19.84%) and the fleet from 27 to 21 vehicles (−22.2%), in 0.42 seconds. All 100 customers are served within capacity and time windows. The acceptance rate of 35.4% sits inside the 20–40% range the literature recommends for VRPTW, a sign the cooling schedule balanced exploring and settling.

What's next

Fewer vehicles means less fuel and fewer drivers, which matters for a volunteer-run program. The next step is comparing against Tabu Search, genetic algorithms and ant colony optimization on the same instance, and running on real depot and recipient coordinates.

04

Solo · Founder & developer · Product · 2026

Kaffa: a management platform for pesantren, growing into an IoT-enabled LMS

Live at Pesantren Bakkatul Munawaroh

The problem

A pesantren runs a dense daily routine for hundreds of students: Quran memorization, congregational prayer attendance, grading, discipline. Most of it lives in WhatsApp messages and scattered spreadsheets. Parents can't follow their child's progress, and teachers type the same data in several places.

What's built

  • One backend, three apps from one Flutter codebase: a guardian app for parents, a teacher app, and a web console for administrators.
  • Multi-tenant by design, with a scope tree (programs, classes, cross-program groups) and roles assigned per scope, so access fails closed instead of leaking.
  • One activity engine reused for memorization, prayer attendance and assessments, instead of one module per activity.
  • Check-in by NFC card or QR code, with manual search when the hardware fails.
  • Dashboards from snapshots, so leaderboards stay fast on low-end phones.

The result

Kaffa is live at Pesantren Bakkatul Munawaroh, and a second pesantren is next. I built, deployed and maintain it on my own.

What's next

Going beyond CRUD. I've written the blueprint for real-time student tracking: BLE tags and gateways feeding a WebGIS, with zone-level presence, exit alerts and per-role access. Gateway placement is treated as an integer program with probabilistic coverage, and a context engine joins location with the pesantren's timetable, so staff see "at the mosque during prayer time" instead of dots on a map. A proof of concept with ten students comes first.
